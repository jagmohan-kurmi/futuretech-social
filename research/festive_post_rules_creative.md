# Festive Social Media Posts for an Indian Digital Marketing Agency (FutureTech, Sagar MP): Creative Best Practice and Legal/Cultural Rules (2025-2026)

Research date: 2 Nov 2026. Sources are mostly trade press, law-firm commentary and platform help pages. Primary legal texts were checked where reachable (Emblems Act Schedule via a bare-act mirror). This is not legal advice. Items marked "seek legal advice" need a lawyer's review before relying on them.

## Q1. Why generic "Happy Diwali" posts underperform and which festive creative approaches work better (with Indian examples)

### Takeaway
Indian trade press and creative leaders consistently say festive creative has become interchangeable (same diyas, sweets, reunions, emotional film). Brand greetings get lost in "a hundred Greetings from XYZ" posts, and consumers barely notice whether a brand posts one at all. The campaigns that stood out were useful to the audience, specific (local, regional or trend-based), or built around customers' businesses. Cadbury's "Not Just a Cadbury Ad" is the closest model for an agency that serves local SMBs. No controlled engagement study comparing generic greetings with value-led festive posts was found; the case rests on expert opinion and award entries.

### Cited Findings
**The sameness problem**
- White Rivers Media strategist Adrine D'mello calls Diwali sameness "a strategy failure more than a craft one": the same diyas, sweets, gatherings and gifting appear in nearly every campaign. The "emotional film with a message" is so overused that audiences can predict its emotional beat — [exchange4media, "Who owns Diwali"](https://www.exchange4media.com/festive-season-news/who-owns-diwali-the-brands-the-culture-or-the-consumer-159612.html)
- Creative heads disagreed on whether Diwali 2026 ads stood out. Experts said most were "overly product-centric, with a transactional feel", and a handful stood out through more nuanced storytelling — [Social Samosa, "Did Diwali ads stand out this year?"](https://socialsamosa.com/experts-speak/did-diwali-ads-stand-out-this-year-creative-heads-disagree-7564589)
- A column describes corporate festive greetings as "drowned in noise", lost among a hundred "Greetings from XYZ Fertilisers" posts — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/analysis/indian-corporates-and-their-grand-ritual-of-festival-branding)
- Social Samosa (2017) argues that brands post greetings out of fear of missing out on what competitors do, and that consumers barely notice whether a brand wishes them on Diwali, Eid or 15 August — [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/2017/10/brands-festive-marketing)
- A guest post faults campaigns for showing one generic North Indian Diwali (crackers, lights, sweets) and ignoring regional traditions. It urges showing how people really celebrate, imperfections included — [Social Samosa guest post](https://socialsamosa.com/guest-post/what-missing-diwali-ads-lets-talk-7368749)

**Examples that worked and why**
- **Cadbury Celebrations "#NotJustACadburyAd" (Diwali 2020, Ogilvy Mumbai and Wavemaker):** AI and pin-code targeting produced thousands of versions of one Diwali ad, each naming the viewer's nearby local shops. The aim was to help small retailers hit by the pandemic. Reported reach was about 1,800 local shops across 260 to 271 pin codes. Reported results include 32% business growth over plan per Think with Google (LBB cites +29% vs last year), 16,000 new stores in traditional trade, and a 40% view-through rate against a 5% benchmark. These are self-reported award figures and the sources differ — [Think with Google](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/intl/en-apac/future-of-marketing/creativity/purpose-driven-marketing-mondelez-india); [WPP](https://www.wpp.com/featured/work/ogilvy-and-wavemaker-notjustacadburyad); [Cannes Lions entry](https://www.lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/not-just-a-cadbury-ad-710095); [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/why-cadbury-shared-its-diwali-ad-with-local-stores-through-a-first-of-a-kind-use-of-ai-108976.html)
- **Swiggy "Why Is This A Swiggy Ad?" (Diwali 2022):** Swiggy skipped the Diwali media rush and ran a puzzle campaign across billboards, front-page print and social, reminding people of its existing relevance rather than competing for space. The source is an award entry, so results are self-reported — [Cannes Lions entry](https://www.lovethework.com/work-awards/entries/why-is-this-a-swiggy-ad-674956)
- **Trend riffing:** Swiggy and Zomato posted playful takes on the "Soan Papdi" Diwali-gift meme, and brands "preach, teach and stay quirky" on Diwali social — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/brands-preach-teach-and-stay-quirky-this-diwali-on-social-media-100467.html)
- A Swiggy marketer said moment marketing works when "done in a controlled manner": be clear about which moment you are marketing, and use social listening to keep content from going stale — same [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/advertising-news/brands-preach-teach-and-stay-quirky-this-diwali-on-social-media-100467.html)
- Amul is cited as the long-running leader of topical "moment" creatives — [The Media Ant](https://www.themediaant.com/blog/cultural-moment-marketing-guide) (blog, secondary)
- Sprinklr data showed about 155K social mentions for Zomato and 145K for Swiggy in the 2024 Diwali season. That measures attention, not conversion — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/brand-makers/indias-social-media-buzz-whatsapp-amazon-netflix-and-ola-emerge-as-top-mentions-in-2024-50072.htm)
- Tanishq's #PehliWaliDiwali is listed among top Diwali social campaigns. It is a roundup mention with no performance data — [Shiprocket](https://shiprocket.in/blog/diwali-social-media-campaigns)

**Hooks for useful festive tips (local SEO)**
- Google Business Profile lets businesses set **special hours** for holidays and events, after regular hours exist. Closures of six days or fewer should use special hours; longer closures should be marked temporarily closed. **Offer posts** can carry a start and end date, so they stay live longer than the 7-day life of Update posts. Event posts cover in-store events. Guides advise adding holiday hours a few weeks ahead, or up to 4 weeks ahead — [Google Business Profile help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/15300403?hl=en-GB); [BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-business-profile-holiday-ready/); [Milestone](https://blog.milestoneinternet.com/web-design-promotion/holiday-season-is-here-drive-search-visibility-with-gmb-special-hours-attributes/amp)
- Google says 7 in 10 festive shoppers who see a product on social go to Google Search to research it. 9 in 10 Navratri/Diwali buyers researched first, yet 70% also made unplanned purchases (Ipsos study commissioned by Google, published 8 Oct 2026) — [Google India blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/google-companies/decoding-indias-two-speed-festive-shopper-how-ai-search-and-youtube-drive-the-cart/); [MediaInfoline](https://www.mediainfoline.com/article/9-in-10-shoppers-research-extensively-yet-70-buy-on-impulse)

### Inferences
- For FutureTech, the Cadbury logic scales down well: a "Sagar ke local dukaan" festive series that features actual clients (with their consent), built on the theme "Diwali par aapke customer aapko Google par dhoondh rahe hain". That positions the agency as the helper of local business, not just another greeter.
- Content pillars that follow from the evidence:
  - (a) Useful festive checklists, e.g. "Diwali sale se pehle Google Business Profile mein special hours + Offer post daalein", "billing software mein festive GST invoice / discount setup", "website par festive banner + WhatsApp button".
  - (b) Client-business festive templates as a showcase: a sweet shop, jeweller or kirana creative made by FutureTech.
  - (c) Behind the scenes: the team at Diwali rush, client launches before Dhanteras.
  - (d) Regional flavour: Bundelkhandi or Sagar references, Lakha Banjara Lake, local melas, Hindi/Hinglish copy.
  - (e) One plain, tasteful greeting on the day itself, branded and brief.
- Because social drives search, festive social posts should push people to a findable destination (GBP, website, WhatsApp), which also ties naturally to the agency's services.

### Gaps
- No controlled or quantitative study was found showing generic greeting posts get lower engagement than value-led festive posts in India. The claim rests on expert opinion.
- No verified 2025-2026 festive campaign case study from a small or mid-size Indian agency was found.
- Zomato/Amul 2025-2026 specific festive posts were not individually verified.

## Q2. Branding rules for festive posts (logo, colours, motifs, consistency, template system)

### Takeaway
No authoritative Indian source sets festive branding rules. Best practice here is inferred from the sameness critique in Q1: the brand must stay recognisable when every feed is full of the same diyas.

### Cited Findings
- Festive creative converges on the same visual codes (diyas, sweets, gatherings), which makes undifferentiated posts interchangeable — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/festive-season-news/who-owns-diwali-the-brands-the-culture-or-the-consumer-159612.html)
- Regional authenticity is valued over generic pan-India imagery — [Social Samosa guest post](https://socialsamosa.com/guest-post/what-missing-diwali-ads-lets-talk-7368749)

### Inferences (practitioner guidance, not sourced rules)
- **Template system:** one master festive frame per platform size (1080x1350 feed, 1080x1920 story/reel cover). It keeps a fixed logo position (for example top-left or bottom-right, never over the motif), a brand-colour base (FutureTech blue/cyan) with festival accent colours added as highlights rather than replacing brand colours, one festival motif layer that changes per festival, a headline zone, and a fixed footer for website, phone/WhatsApp and handle.
- **Clutter limits:** one message per post, at most about 3 text elements, motif at no more than about 30% of the canvas, generous whitespace, and Hindi/Hinglish headline readable on mobile.
- **Client version:** the same frame with the client's logo and colours, plus a small "Designed by FutureTech" credit only if the client agrees.
- **Consistency:** a festive calendar ensures every festival (Diwali, Holi, Eid, Christmas, Guru Nanak Jayanti, Makar Sankranti, Independence and Republic Day) gets the same template treatment, which also supports inclusivity (see Q4).

### Gaps
- No Indian industry standard or study on festive logo placement or clutter was found. The guidance above is inference.

## Q3. Festive offers: truthful "limited-time" framing under ASCI, the CCPA 2022 misleading-ads guidelines and the CCPA 2023 dark-patterns guidelines

### Takeaway
"Limited offer" and "only X left" claims must be true and provable. ASCI treats artificial scarcity as misleading and puts the burden on the advertiser to show stock levels justified the claim. The CCPA's 2023 Dark Patterns Guidelines list "false urgency" as a prohibited dark pattern. The CCPA's 2022 Misleading Ads Guidelines restrict "free" claims, bait ads and contradictory disclaimers, with penalties of up to Rs 10 lakh, plus endorser bans. Influencer or collab posts need a prominent #ad / #collab label in every post.

### Cited Findings
**ASCI (self-regulatory)**
- ASCI's Guidelines on Online Deceptive Design Patterns in Advertising took effect on **1 Sep 2023**. They cover drip pricing, bait and switch, false urgency and disguised ads — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/viewpoint-asci-guidelines-deceptive-design-patterns-digital-media-advertisements); [Khaitan & Co](https://www.khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/ASCI-guidelines-on-deceptive-design-patterns-in-digital-media-advertisements); [Fox Mandal](https://foxmandal.in/news/asci-releases-guidelines-to-curb-dark-patterns-in-online-advertising/)
- **False urgency:** stating or implying that quantities are more limited than they are (artificial scarcity) is misleading. If a complaint is raised, the advertiser must show that the stock position when the "limited quantity" message appeared made the claim reasonable. This is from search summaries of the law-firm coverage above; ASCI's own text was not fetched — [Fox Mandal](https://foxmandal.in/news/asci-releases-guidelines-to-curb-dark-patterns-in-online-advertising/); [K&S Partners](https://ksandk.com/newsletter/asci-releases-guidelines-to-combat-dark-patterns-in-online-advertising/)
- An ASCI review found that 29% of 2021-22 ads it processed were disguised ads, mostly influencer posts that concealed paid content — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/viewpoint-asci-guidelines-deceptive-design-patterns-digital-media-advertisements)
- **Influencer disclosure:** permitted labels are #ad, #collab, #promo, #sponsored and #partnership, placed in the first two lines of the caption or otherwise prominently. A bio disclosure does not count, and the platform's paid-partnership tool alone is not sufficient. Advertiser and influencer should sign an agreement covering disclosure and due diligence — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/new-influencer-marketing-guidelines-mandating-disclosure-on-promotional-content-to-be-effective-from-june-14/); [Nishith Desai](https://www.nishithdesai.com/default.aspx?id=4713)
- A 2025 addendum targets health, nutrition and BFSI creators. A 2026 update reportedly covers AI virtual influencers. These are secondary claims; verify on ascionline.in — [SansaLegal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/asci-influencer-advertising-guidelines-2026-disclosure-rules-for-paid-content-and-ai-influencers); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/influencer-marketing-sector-demands-attention-asci-85507.htm)
- ASCI codes are not legally binding for most digital advertising, except on ASCI members, but enforcement bodies such as the CCPA rely on them — [Nishith Desai](https://www.nishithdesai.com/default.aspx?id=4713); [Fox Mandal](https://foxmandal.in/news/asci-releases-guidelines-to-curb-dark-patterns-in-online-advertising/)

**CCPA Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements, 2022 (under the Consumer Protection Act 2019)**
- Notified on 9 June 2022. Sources differ on whether they took effect on 9 or 10 June 2022 — [Nishith Desai](https://www.nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/technology-law-analysis/guidelines-on-misleading-advertisements-and-endorsements-notified-under-the-consumer-protection-act-2019-6172); [AZB](https://www.azbpartners.com/?p=24774)
- **Bait ads** are not banned but come with conditions; for example, the product must actually be available at the advertised price — [Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/CCPA-releases-guidelines-for-Prevention-of-Misleading-Advertisements-and-Endorsements-for-Misleading-Advertisements-2022)
- **"Free" claims** must not require the consumer to pay anything beyond the unavoidable cost of responding and collecting — [Vikaspedia text](https://en.vikaspedia.in/social-welfare/social-awareness/consumer-education/guidelines-for-prevention-of-misleading-advertisements-and-endorsements-for-misleading-advertisements-2022)
- **Disclaimers** may clarify a claim but must not contradict the main claim or try to correct a misleading one. They must be in the same language as the claim, clear and prominent — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/social-media/1312524/ccpa-guidelines-for-misleading-advertisements-and-endorsements-2022); [Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan](https://cn.lakshmisri.com/insights/articles/prevention-of-misleading-advertisements-analysis-of-guidelines-issued-by-central-consumer-protection-authority)
- **Penalties:** up to Rs 10 lakh on manufacturers, advertisers and endorsers, with higher amounts for repeat violations. Endorsers can be barred for up to 1 year (longer for repeats) — [Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/CCPA-releases-guidelines-for-Prevention-of-Misleading-Advertisements-and-Endorsements-for-Misleading-Advertisements-2022)

**CCPA Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023**
- Notified on 30 Nov 2023. Annexure 1 lists 13 dark patterns with illustrations, including **false urgency** (creating a false sense of urgency or scarcity to push an immediate purchase), basket sneaking, confirm shaming, forced action, subscription trap, bait and switch, drip pricing and disguised ads — [JSA](https://www.jsalaw.com/newsletters-and-updates/ccpa-issues-guidelines-for-prevention-and-regulation-of-dark-patterns-2023/); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2023/12/04/ccpa-notifies-guidelines-for-prevention-and-regulation-of-dark-patterns-2023-legal-news/); [Mondaq overview](https://www.mondaq.com/article/1844140/ccpas-guidelines-on-dark-patterns-an-overview)
- The illustrations are guidance only and may vary case by case, per a 2026 commentary — [Mondaq overview](https://www.mondaq.com/article/1844140/ccpas-guidelines-on-dark-patterns-an-overview)
- A CCPA advisory dated 5 June 2025 directed e-commerce platforms and digital advertisers to self-audit their practices, including influencer marketing — [SansaLegal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/asci-influencer-advertising-guidelines-2026-disclosure-rules-for-paid-content-and-ai-influencers) (secondary)

### Inferences
- **Offer-copy rules for FutureTech and client posts:**
  - State real dates, e.g. "Offer valid 20 Oct – 3 Nov 2026", and honour them; do not "extend" a "last day" offer repeatedly.
  - Use "Only 10 slots" only if capacity really is 10, and keep a record such as a booking sheet as proof.
  - No fake countdown timers on landing pages.
  - Show the full price including GST, with no drip pricing.
  - "Free website audit" must have no hidden charge.
  - Key conditions belong in the main caption or creative in the same language, not in a tiny contradicting footnote.
- Client paid partnerships and collab posts with local influencers need #ad or #collab in the first two lines.
- The dark-patterns guidelines are written for "persons including platforms, advertisers and sellers". I believe this is their stated scope but did not fetch the text to confirm it. An agency that writes the copy could be exposed as advertiser or endorser alongside the client. **Seek legal advice** on agency liability and on client contract indemnity clauses.

### Gaps
- ASCI's primary text (ascionline.in) and the CCPA gazette PDFs were not fetched directly. Exact wording of the burden-of-proof clause and the Annexure illustrations should be verified.
- The 2026 ASCI influencer update details are unverified.

## Q4. Religious and cultural sensitivity; national symbols (Emblems Act, Flag Code, PM/leader images)

### Takeaway
Festive ads that touch inter-faith themes or "re-name" Hindu festivals have drawn intense boycott campaigns: Tanishq withdrew its ad in 2020 and Fabindia in 2021. BNS s.299 (formerly IPC s.295A) criminalises deliberate, malicious insult to religious feelings, including through visible representations and electronic means. Intent is required, but complaints alone cause disruption. The Emblems Act bars commercial use of the national flag, government emblems, the Ashoka Chakra, and the names or pictures of the PM, Mahatma Gandhi, Nehru and Shivaji without central government permission. Reliance Jio and Paytm had to apologise for using the PM's photo in ads.

### Cited Findings
**Controversies**
- **Tanishq "Ekatvam" (Oct 2020):** the ad showed an inter-faith couple's baby shower. It was trolled as promoting "love jihad", and Tanishq disabled comments and then withdrew it, saying it had "stimulated divergent and severe reactions" — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Ftanishq-trolled-for-promoting-love-jihad-withdraws-advertisement-901291.html); [Outlook](https://outlookindia.com/national/india-news-tanishq-ad-withdrawn-amid-trolling-boycott-calls-news-362106); [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2020/10/15/tata-group-s-jewellery-brand-runs-trouble-with-its-multi-faith-ad)
- **Fabindia "Jashn-e-Riwaaz" (Oct 2021):** critics, including BJP leaders, objected to an Urdu name for a Diwali-season collection, and Fabindia pulled the promo — [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/2021/10/fabindia-pulls-down-festive-ad-after-social-backlash-over-usage-of-jashn-e-riwaaz); [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/trolled-fabindia-retracts-advert-jashn-e-riwaaz-326808/amp)
- Deepavali 2021 was described as a "season of outrage", with outrage also over models without a bindi — [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/news/fabindia-where-bindi-deepavali-has-been-season-outrage-156793)
- A column mocks careless deity imagery in corporate greetings, such as "Lord Shiva playing a guitar under the Northern Lights" — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/analysis/indian-corporates-and-their-grand-ritual-of-festival-branding)

**BNS s.299**
- It punishes deliberate and malicious acts intended to outrage the religious feelings of any class by words, signs, visible representations, or electronic means. It replaces IPC s.295A, carries up to 3 years' imprisonment and/or a fine, and requires deliberate malicious intent, not mere hurt sentiment — [Devgan BNS s.299](https://devgan.in/bns/section/299/); [LawRato](https://lawrato.com/bharatiya-nyaya-sanhita/bns-section-299)
- Sources conflict on bailability. One says prior government sanction (BNSS s.217) is needed before a court takes cognisance — [Vidhi Judicial](https://www.vidhijudicial.com/section-299-of-the-bharatiya-nyaya-sanhita-(bns)-of-2023.html); [SuperKalam](https://superkalam.com/current-affairs/articles/section-299-bns-vs-section-295a-ipc-key-differences-explained)

**Emblems and Names (Prevention of Improper Use) Act, 1950**
- s.3 bars using any Schedule item for trade, business or professional purposes without central government permission. Schedule items include:
  - (3) the Indian National Flag
  - (4) the name, emblem or seal of the Government of India or any State
  - (6) the name or seal of the President and Governors
  - (7) names suggesting government patronage
  - (9) Rashtrapati and Rashtrapati Bhavan
  - (9-A) the names or pictures of Mahatma Gandhi, Jawaharlal Nehru, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj and the **Prime Minister of India**, and the words "Gandhi", "Nehru" and "Shivaji" (with a narrow calendar exception)
  - (10) government medals
  - (16) the Ashoka Chakra / Dharma Chakra as used in the flag or state seals
  - (17) Parliament, Supreme Court and High Court names or buildings
  — [AdvocateKhoj Schedule](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/emblemsandnames/schedule.php); [India Code PDF](https://indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1896/5/A1950-12.pdf)
- News reports put the PM entry at "Entry 11" while bare-act copies list it as 9-A, so cite 9-A from the Act text — [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/amp/story/news/politics/prime-minister-narendra-modi-photo-advertisement-permission-rules-violated); [Tilak Marg](https://tilakmarg.com/acts/emblems-and-names-prevention-of-improper-use-act-1950/)
- Reliance Jio and Paytm apologised after using the PM's photo in ads without permission. The fine was reported as only Rs 500 (not verified against the Act text) — [TelecomTalk](https://telecomtalk.info/?p=161346); [The Wire](https://thewire.in/115725/sorry-say-paytm-reliance-jio-modi-advertisements/); [Tilak Marg](https://tilakmarg.com/news/reliance-jio-using-pm-modi-in-advertisements-is-prohibited-in-law-but-with-fine-of-%E2%82%B9-500-only/)

**Flag Code of India 2002**
- The flag "shall not be used for commercial purposes in violation of the Emblems Act". No advertisement or lettering may be put on the flag — [ClearIAS](https://www.clearias.com/flag-code-of-india/); [Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/education/childrens-corner/flag-code-of-india)
- An order of 30 Dec 2021 allowed machine-made and polyester flags. The July 2022 amendment allowed day-and-night display (Har Ghar Tiranga). Neither changed the commercial-use bar — [EastMojo](https://eastmojo.com/national-news/2022/07/23/govt-tweaks-flag-code-now-can-be-flown-day-night/); [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/flag-code-of-india/print_manually)

### Inferences
- **Do's for Independence and Republic Day posts:** abstract tricolour colour bands or patterns, freedom-themed messages and the brand's own illustration.
- **Don'ts:**
  - no realistic national flag carrying a logo, offer or text
  - no Ashoka Chakra or State Emblem (lion capital)
  - no flag inverted, distorted, used as a background for "sale %", or worn as clothing
  - no PM, President, Gandhi or Nehru photos or names, and no "Govt approved" style claims
  - no political party colours or slogans
- **Deities:** avoid putting deities in sale or discount creatives, and never place a logo, price or "% off" on a deity image or alter it humorously. Prefer neutral motifs (diya, rangoli, lantern, crescent, kite) over deity images; where a deity is appropriate (e.g. a Ganesh Chaturthi greeting), use a respectful, licensed or original illustration with no commercial overlay.
- **Inclusivity:** greet across festivals with equal template quality. Avoid commentary on inter-faith themes and on festival "renaming" unless a client consciously chooses it with legal and PR review. Do not engage trolls from the brand handle. Keep a takedown and escalation plan.
- **Seek legal advice** before any campaign involving religious themes, national symbols or political figures, and before responding to any FIR or legal notice.

### Gaps
- No case law was found applying BNS s.299 specifically to advertising or deity imagery.
- The Emblems Act penalty amount was not verified from primary text.
- No source was found on ASCI complaints specifically about deity images in festive ads.

## Q5. Copyright and image rights: deity art, stock, Pinterest, AI images, Reels music, fonts

### Takeaway
Treat everything as copyrighted unless licensed. Business accounts should use Meta's Sound Collection, which is royalty-free for commercial use but only on Meta's platforms, not trending film songs. Reels with copyrighted music reportedly cannot be boosted. Pinterest is a discovery tool, not a licence. Purely AI-generated images are likely not copyrightable in India (human authorship needed), and the user bears infringement risk if outputs resemble existing works.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram's Sound Collection offers 14,000+ royalty-free songs and sounds for Reels and Stories that can be used commercially, including in ads — [Instagram Help](https://help.instagram.com/ipad-app/402084904469945); [Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/meta-offers-royalty-free-music-library-to-instagram-reels-advertisers)
- The licence covers only content on Meta's products. The same tracks cannot be reused on YouTube, a website or WhatsApp-forwarded video files outside Meta — [Fox Music](https://www.foximusic.com/blog/instagram-reels-music-copyright-legal-guide/); [Artyfile](https://artyfile.com/music-for-reels)
- Business accounts get a restricted music library because Meta's popular-music agreements cover personal use. Using copyrighted music can lead to muted audio, blocked posts or reduced reach — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/instagram-music-library); [Hello Thematic](https://hellothematic.com/music-rules-tiktok-instagram-youtube/)
- Reels with copyrighted music reportedly cannot be boosted as ads, per a quoted Meta message (vendor source) — [Artyfile](https://artyfile.com/music-for-reels)
- **AI images:** Indian copyright law (Copyright Act 1957) is generally read as requiring human authorship, and there is no specific ruling or amendment for AI works. A tool's terms granting a licence do not create copyright, most tools do not indemnify users, and outputs "substantially similar" to existing works (e.g. Ghibli-style) may infringe. Fair dealing is narrow for commercial use — [K&S Partners](https://ksandk.com/intellectual-property-rights/ai-generated-content-copyright-ownership-india/); [Khurana & Khurana](https://www.khuranaandkhurana.com/ownership-of-ai-generated-content-a-deep-dive-into-copyright-law-in-india); [BOOM](https://www.boomlive.in/law/openai-chatgpt-imitates-studio-ghibli-style-for-social-media-photos-28240)
- Documenting human creative input (editing, composition, sign-off) strengthens any authorship claim — [ATB Legal](https://atblegal.com/blog/intellectual-property-laww/can-you-copyright-ai-generated-art/)

### Inferences
- **Pinterest and Google Images:** these are reference only. Use licensed stock (Freepik, Shutterstock or Adobe Stock with a commercial licence, keeping licence records) or original design.
- **Deity artwork:** traditional deities are not themselves copyrighted, but specific paintings, photos and illustrations by artists are. Use licensed or original art.
- **Fonts:** use fonts licensed for commercial use, such as Google Fonts (open licences) or paid licences; check Devanagari fonts too.
- **AI images:** check each tool's commercial-use terms, avoid named-artist styles and real people, check for distorted hands or text and for wrong religious details, and keep a human edit.
- **Reels:** use Sound Collection or original or licensed audio for any post that may be boosted.
- **Seek legal advice** for paid national campaigns using AI imagery or music outside the Sound Collection.

## Q6. Planning workflow: lead time, day-before vs on-day, Reels vs static, boosting

### Takeaway
Indian festive shoppers plan and research weeks to months ahead: 82% plan in advance, "Diwali sales" searches start rising in August, and Amazon/YouGov found 78% start thinking before September. Festive offer content should therefore start 3-6 weeks before the festival, with GBP hours and offers set about 2-4 weeks ahead. Greeting posts belong on the day or the evening before. No India-specific source compared Reels with static posts or day-before with on-day posting.

### Inferences (suggested workflow for FutureTech)
- **T-8 to T-6 weeks:** festive calendar for the agency and each client; template frames; licensed asset folder; offer terms and dates agreed in writing with each client.
- **T-6 to T-3 weeks:** "get ready for Diwali" value content (GBP update, website festive banner, billing software setup, Meta/Google festive ads); client offer creatives approved; GBP special hours and Offer posts scheduled.
- **T-3 weeks to T-1 day:** offer posts, Reels (BTS, client showcases, tips) and boosted posts.
  - Boost value or offer content, not plain greetings.
  - Start ads early because auction costs rise near the festival. This rests on general industry knowledge and is not sourced here.
- **Day before or on the day:** one branded greeting (static or a short Reel), with no hard sell. Monitor comments.
- **T+1 week:** thank-you or results post (e.g. "client ki Diwali leads"), with permission.
- **Format:** use Reels for reach (tips, BTS, showcase) and static or carousel for checklists and offers. Test both; no India-specific data was found.
